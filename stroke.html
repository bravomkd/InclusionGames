Stroke Rehab

Camera-guided movement practice for people recovering from a stroke. No keyboard, no mouse, no controller — the camera watches the movement and the exercise responds to it.

Please read first

This is practice software to use alongside your rehabilitation, not instead of it. It is not a medical device, it does not diagnose anything, and it cannot tell you whether an exercise is right for you. Ask your physiotherapist, occupational therapist or doctor which of these to do and how often.

Sit down and supported before you start. Move slowly, and only as far as is comfortable. Stop straight away if anything hurts, or if you feel dizzy, breathless or unwell.

Video never leaves the device. The camera is processed in this browser only. No video and no biometric data is uploaded or stored — only the exercise results, such as how many repetitions were completed.

What these exercises are based on

Each exercise rests on a principle that is well established in stroke rehabilitation rather than on a claim about this particular software. In short:

  • Repetition matters. Motor recovery is driven by many correct, actively-initiated repetitions of a task. Counting them is most of what these exercises do.
  • Difficulty has to match the person. Each exercise measures your own range first, then sets the target as a share of what you actually showed — never against an able-bodied norm.
  • Feedback helps learning. Seeing the movement live, and seeing the two sides of the face compared, is the standard rationale for mirror-based facial retraining.
  • Scanning practice for one-sided inattention. Targets are placed towards the side that is easy to miss. This reliably improves searching in tasks like this; how far it carries into everyday life is less certain, and is a judgement for your clinician.

Interactive and virtual-reality training of this kind is generally studied as an addition to conventional therapy. The honest summary is that it is a reasonable way to get extra practice between appointments — not a replacement for the appointments.
